# Sustainable plan design and longitudinal fatigue

Use this when an athlete reports recurring fatigue, repeated poor-quality sessions, or frustration that the plan requires weekly improvisation.

## Core interpretation

- Treat recurring subjective fatigue as a plan-design signal even when sleep, HRV, resting HR, nutrition, and medical screening look normal.
- Review the longitudinal load trajectory, not just today's TSB: CTL ramp, ATL peaks, consecutive negative TSB days, hard-session density, and actual-vs-planned load.
- A rapidly rising CTL can represent a rapidly rising training demand; a negative TSB is context, not a diagnosis.
- Distinguish a healthy, deliberate overload block from an unsustainably dense schedule. If the athlete repeatedly says sessions feel like a struggle, the schedule is not currently executable.

## Plan design response

Build a plan that can run largely unchanged:

1. Start with a lower sustainable baseline than the athlete's recent peak load.
2. Use one key intensity session per week initially when fatigue is recurrent; add a second only after several stable weeks.
3. Keep the long run easy and non-progressive during rebuilding.
4. Separate hard sessions by at least 48 hours and place easy/rest days explicitly.
5. Schedule a deload every 3rd week (or sooner when the longitudinal trend warrants it), rather than waiting for failure.
6. Increase volume conservatively and avoid simultaneous increases in volume, intensity, and strength.
7. Present the complete revised week/block before bulk-editing the calendar.

## Communication

Acknowledge the athlete's constraint directly: they should not have to renegotiate the plan every week. Explain that adaptation belongs in the plan, not in repeated emergency substitutions. Avoid framing normal fatigue as a motivation problem or diagnosing overtraining from TSB alone.

## Data caveat

Wellness CTL/ATL/TSB may be sport-scoped or mislabeled depending on the platform/plugin response. Always reconcile it with sport-specific activities and settings before attributing the load to running or cycling. If a stats query fails, do not imply a precise aggregate; state which data was actually available and retry with corrected parameters when needed.
